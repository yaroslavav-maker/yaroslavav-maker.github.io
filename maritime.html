In maritime English, numbers are pronounced clearly to avoid confusion. Pay attention to special pronunciations!

🌊 Maritime Context Examples

Depth: "Depth 15 meters" → "Depth one-five meters"

Speed: "Speed 12 knots" → "Speed one-two knots"

Coordinates: "Position 54°30'N" → "Position five-four degrees three-zero minutes North"

📝 Number Pronunciation Rules

1

🗣️ Say digits separately

Never say "fifteen" for 15 in coordinates or depths. Say "one-five".

15 meters → "one-five meters" (NOT "fifteen meters")
2

🔤 Special pronunciations

Some numbers have special forms to avoid confusion on radio:

3 → "tree"
4 → "fower"
5 → "fife"
9 → "niner"
3

⚓ Decimal points

Use "decimal" or "point" clearly:

3.5 meters → "three decimal five meters"

All maritime communications use UTC (Coordinated Universal Time). Time is always spoken in 24-hour format, digit by digit.

14:30 UTC

📝 Time Communication Rules

1

⏰ Always use 24-hour format

Never use AM/PM in maritime communications.

2:30 PM → 14:30 → "one-four three-zero"
2

🔤 Say each digit separately

Time is always pronounced digit by digit, not as a number.

09:15 → "zero-niner one-five"
23:45 → "two-three four-five"
3

🌍 Always specify UTC

Always add "UTC" or "Zulu time" after the time to avoid confusion.

"ETA 1830 UTC" → "Estimated time of arrival one-eight three-zero UTC"

📝 Verb Usage Rules

1

✅ Use imperative for instructions

In urgent situations, use direct commands (imperative form).

"Stop engine!"
"Alter course to starboard!"
"Reduce speed!"
2

🗣️ Use present simple for reports

When reporting current situation, use present simple tense.

"I am flooding" (NOT "I flood")
"We are drifting" (NOT "We drift")
3

🔄 Use "will" for intentions

When stating your next action, use "will + verb".

"I will alter course to port"
"We will reduce speed to 8 knots"
